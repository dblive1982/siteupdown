Online banking or a payment site not working? What to do safely

Money makes outages stressful. The risks are paying twice and falling for scams that appear during problems. The safe approach is to slow down, confirm the problem, and check before trying again.

If a payment fails or freezes halfway

  1. Do not press pay again straight away. The first attempt may have succeeded and only the confirmation failed.
  2. Check your bank app or statement for a pending charge, and your email for an order confirmation.
  3. Wait 10 to 15 minutes before trying again. Pending charges that never turn into an order are usually released automatically within a few days.
  4. If you were charged but have no order, contact the shop with the time, amount and any reference number.

If the bank site or app will not load

  • Use the checker to see whether the bank’s site is up for everyone. Banks also have separate systems for the app, card payments and cash machines, which can fail independently.
  • Try the other route: if the website fails, try the app, and the reverse.
  • Check the bank’s official status page or its verified social accounts.
  • Do not use a link from a text or email to get there. Type the address or use the official app.

Common causes on your side

  • An out-of-date app or browser.
  • A VPN, which many banks block for security (see VPNs and websites that stop working).
  • A wrong date or time on your device, which breaks the secure connection.
  • A security warning on the page. Treat it seriously and read SSL certificate errors. Never enter card details on a page showing one.

Staying safe during an outage

Outages attract scammers. Be wary of any message saying a service is down and asking you to “verify your account”, call a number, or move money to a “safe account”. A real bank will not ask for your full PIN or password, and will not ask you to transfer money to protect it. If in doubt, end the contact and call the number on the back of your card.

A worked example

You pay for tickets and the page shows an error after you click Pay. The checker shows the ticket site is up, and your banking app shows a pending charge. You wait 15 minutes and your email brings the confirmation. If you had paid again, you would have been charged twice. If no confirmation arrived, you would contact the seller with the reference from the pending charge.

Prevention

  • Keep a second payment method available for important purchases.
  • For urgent payments, avoid the last minute, so an outage does not matter.
  • Keep your banking apps updated and enable alerts for transactions.

This guide is general information, not financial advice. For problems with money already taken, your bank or card provider is the right place to start.
