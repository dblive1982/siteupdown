Why Netflix, YouTube or other streaming keeps buffering

Buffering happens when video arrives more slowly than it plays. The cause can be the service, your home network, your internet connection or the device itself. Working out which saves a lot of time.

How much speed streaming needs

  • Standard definition: about 3 Mbps.
  • HD (1080p): about 5 to 8 Mbps.
  • 4K: about 15 to 25 Mbps.

These are per stream. A household watching on two TVs while someone downloads a game needs much more. Speed is only part of the story: a stable connection with low packet loss matters as much as a high number. See latency, packet loss and jitter.

Work out where the problem is

  1. Is it one service or all of them? If only one app buffers while others stream fine, the problem is probably that service or its delivery network. Use the checker.
  2. Is it one device or all of them? If a phone streams fine but the TV buffers, look at the TV: its Wi-Fi signal, its app version or its memory.
  3. Is it a certain time of day? Buffering every evening at peak hours points to congestion on your provider’s network, which they can fix or explain.
  4. Run a speed test on the same Wi-Fi, ideally close to the router. Our internet speed test also shows latency. Compare the result with your plan.

Common causes and fixes

  • Weak Wi-Fi signal: walls and distance cut speed sharply. Move the router, use a wired cable to the TV, or add a mesh unit.
  • Busy 2.4 GHz band: the 5 GHz band is faster at close range. Use it for TVs near the router.
  • Too many devices: pause big downloads and cloud backups while streaming.
  • An old router: routers that are many years old can struggle with several HD streams.
  • VPN on: a VPN adds distance and overhead. Turn it off to test.
  • Service-side problems: a delivery network issue can hit one service in one region while the rest of the internet is fine.

A worked example

A family pays for a 100 Mbps plan but the living-room TV buffers at 8 pm. A speed test beside the router shows 95 Mbps, but the same test in the living room shows 9 Mbps. The plan is fine; the Wi-Fi signal does not reach the TV. Running a cable or adding a mesh unit fixes it, and no call to the provider is needed.

If the service is the problem

Most platforms let you lower the quality manually. Dropping from 4K to HD cuts the speed needed to a third and is a useful workaround during a partial outage. Remember that large outages show up at once for many people; check the outage map to see whether others are affected.

When to call your provider

Call if speed is far below your plan when wired directly to the router, if it drops at the same time every day, or if the connection keeps disconnecting. Give them speed test results with times, which makes the conversation far shorter. See is it my Wi-Fi, router or ISP.
