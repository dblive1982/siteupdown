How to tell if a website is down for everyone or just you

When a page will not load, the first question is whose problem it is. These four steps answer it in about a minute.

Step 1: ask a checker

Enter the address into the siteupdown.com checker. We load it from our own server and from probes in several countries at the same time. If most of them reach the site, it is up and the problem is likely on your side. If most fail, it is down for everyone.

Step 2: change your network

Turn off Wi-Fi and try on mobile data, or the other way round. If it loads on one but not the other, your network or provider is the cause.

Step 3: change your device or browser

Try another browser, a private window or another device. If that works, the cause is your browser’s saved data or an extension. See how to clear it.

Step 4: check the source

Large services have an official status page or a social media account that announces problems. These are the best source during a big outage, and they are often updated before any checker notices.

Reading the results

  • Up for everyone: every probe reached it. Look at your own connection.
  • Reachable from some places: a regional problem. Your region may be affected.
  • Down for everyone: nothing reaches it. Wait and try again later.
  • Blocking automated checks or no answer: the site may be fine but refuses tools. Open it in your browser to be sure.

Why this is hard to tell from one device

A site can fail for you without being down. The fault can be on your device, your Wi-Fi, your provider, a DNS server, or the site itself. From one browser you cannot tell which, so the quickest test is to ask machines in other places and networks to try it for you. That is what the checker does.

Reading the result

  • Up everywhere: the site is fine. The problem is on your side, so try another network and clear the cache.
  • Down everywhere: the site really is down. Wait, check its status page or social media, and try again later. Nothing you do on your device will fix it.
  • Up in some places, down in others: a regional or routing problem. Read why it’s down for some people.
  • An error code: the site answered but with a problem. A 5xx code is a server fault; a 4xx code is usually about the request or about being blocked. See the status code guide.

A worked example

A game will not log in. The checker shows the website is up, but the login server returns a 503 from every location. That tells you the service is overloaded or in maintenance and that restarting your console or router will not help. You wait and check again in ten minutes.

Why a quick re-check matters

Short outages are common. If a check fails, run it again after a minute: a single failed attempt is not proof. Our system confirms a failure from more than one location before calling a site down for the same reason.
