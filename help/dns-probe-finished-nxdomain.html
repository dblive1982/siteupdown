Fix &ldquo;server not found&rdquo; and DNS_PROBE_FINISHED_NXDOMAIN

Messages such as DNS_PROBE_FINISHED_NXDOMAIN, “Server not found” and “We can’t find that site” all mean the same thing: your device could not turn the website’s name into an address. The website may be fine. The lookup failed.

Why a lookup fails

  • The address was typed wrongly.
  • The domain has expired or has not been set up yet.
  • Your device or router has a stale or broken DNS cache.
  • Your internet provider’s DNS server is having trouble.
  • A VPN, security tool or parental filter is intercepting the lookup.

Fixes, in the order to try them

  1. Check the spelling of the address, including the ending such as .com or .co.uk.
  2. Run a check on siteupdown.com. If our probes reach the site, the problem is on your side. If none do, the site’s DNS or hosting is the problem.
  3. Try another network. Switch from Wi-Fi to mobile data. If it works, your router or provider is the issue.
  4. Restart your router and wait two minutes.
  5. Flush your DNS cache.
  6. Change your DNS server to a public one in your network settings, then try again.
  7. Turn off VPNs and filters for a minute to see whether they are the cause.

If the website is yours

Check that the domain has not expired, the nameservers are correct, and the right A or CNAME record exists. Remember that changes can take a while to spread, as explained in DNS propagation.

What the error really means

NXDOMAIN stands for “non-existent domain”. Your device asked a DNS server for the address of the site and the answer was “no such name”. The browser never even tried to connect, so this is not a slow server or a blocked page. It is a failed name lookup.

Common causes

  • A typo in the address, such as a missing letter or the wrong ending (.com instead of .co.uk).
  • The domain has expired or was never registered. The registry no longer lists it, so every resolver says it does not exist.
  • DNS records were deleted or are being changed. A site owner who moves host can leave a gap where the records are missing.
  • Your resolver is wrong or stale. Your router, provider or a filter returns NXDOMAIN for a site that exists. This is the case where the site works for other people.
  • A security or family filter that deliberately answers “does not exist” for blocked sites.

A worked example

A visitor types exmaple.com and sees the error. The domain they wanted was example.com, so the answer is correct: the misspelt name really does not exist. In a second case, the address is right and works on a phone using mobile data but not on the home Wi-Fi. That points to the router’s DNS, so changing it to a public resolver fixes it.

What to do next

  1. Check the spelling and the domain ending.
  2. Test the same address on another network or device.
  3. Flush your DNS cache.
  4. If you own the site, check that the domain is not expired and that its DNS records exist at your registrar.
  5. If you only changed the records recently, read about DNS propagation; it can take hours.
