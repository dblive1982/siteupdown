Uptime percentages explained: what 99.9% really means

Uptime is the share of time a website is working. It is usually shown as a percentage, and the small differences between the numbers matter more than they look.

What each level allows

This is how much downtime each percentage allows:

UptimePer month (30 days)Per year
99%about 7 hours 12 minutesabout 3 days 15 hours
99.5%about 3 hours 36 minutesabout 1 day 19 hours
99.9%about 43 minutesabout 8 hours 46 minutes
99.99%about 4 minutes 19 secondsabout 52 minutes
99.999%about 26 secondsabout 5 minutes

How it is calculated

Uptime % = (checks that succeeded ÷ total checks) × 100. A monitor that checks every five minutes makes 288 checks a day, so one failed check is about 0.35% of that day. Because checks are spaced out, a short outage between two checks can be missed, and a single failure can be rounded up to five minutes. Treat the figure as an estimate.

Reading a hosting promise

  • Most shared hosting plans promise 99.9%. That is roughly 43 minutes of downtime a month before they owe you anything.
  • Read what is excluded. Planned maintenance, attacks and problems on your own site are usually not counted.
  • The remedy is usually a small credit, not compensation for lost sales.
  • Check the figure yourself instead of relying on the promise. A monitor keeps a 24-hour, 7-day and 30-day record.

How much do you need?

A personal blog is fine at 99.5%. A shop that takes orders around the clock wants 99.9% or better. Each extra nine usually costs much more, because it needs duplicate servers and automatic failover. Choose the level whose downtime you can actually afford.

Averages hide the pattern

Ten minutes of downtime every day and one long outage a month give the same percentage, but customers experience them very differently. Look at the incident list as well as the headline number. Each monitored site’s status page on siteupdown.com shows both.
