Privacy.

We keep this short and plain. siteupdown.com checks whether websites are reachable. We collect as little as we can to do that.

Website checks

When you check an address, our server fetches it. We store only the hostname, the result (status, HTTP code, response time) and the time in a public “recently checked” list. We do not publish who checked it. Regional checks send the hostname you entered to the Globalping probe network, which runs the request from machines around the world.

Share links keep a copy of the result so it can be shown to whoever you send it to. They expire after 30 days.

Accounts

Signing in is optional. If you create an account (with email and password, or with Google) we store your email address, display name and a hashed password (we never see or store your password itself). While you are signed in we also save your checks and your watchlist, and they are private to you. You can reset your data or delete your account at any time from the Account page; deleting removes everything saved with it.

Extra checks

If you add an extra check (a word that must appear on a page, or a port that must answer), we store the website address, the word or port, and the latest result, so we can check it every minute and email you if it fails. You can remove a check at any time on the Monitor page, and deleting your account removes them all.

Browser extension

The siteupdown.com browser extension reads the address of the tab you are on only when you click its toolbar button. It sends just the website name (for example example.com, never the full page address, search terms or page content) to siteupdown.com so we can check whether the site is up. It does not read or store your browsing history, and it does not run in the background. If you are signed in to siteupdown.com in the same browser, the extension uses that sign-in to add a site to your watchlist when you ask it to. Nothing is sold or shared for advertising.

Alert channels

If you connect Telegram, Slack or Discord alerts, we store the Telegram chat number or the webhook address you give us, so we can send you a short message when a site on your watchlist goes down or comes back up. These messages pass through Telegram, Slack or Discord, which have their own privacy policies. You can remove a channel at any time on the Monitor page, send /stop to our Telegram bot, or delete your account to remove them all.

If you sign in with Google, Google tells us your name and verified email address. We do not get access to anything else in your Google account.

Problem reports

After a check, and on the “Is it down?” pages, you can report a problem with a site. A report stores the site, the type of problem, the time, and a one-way hash of your network address, which is used only to limit repeat reports and is erased after two days. The report itself (site, type, time and the country you are in, worked out from your network or your device’s time zone, with nothing that identifies you) is kept for up to three years so the report history can be shown. We do not ask for your name or email address for reports, and reports are shown only as anonymous counts.

Cookies and storage

We use essential storage to run the site: your theme, your cookie choice, your recent searches (kept on your device) and a sign-in cookie if you are signed in. Analytics and advertising cookies are optional and off until you allow them in “Cookie settings”. Google’s advertising script is only loaded if you allow advertising cookies.

Advertising

siteupdown.com is supported by advertising. Third-party vendors, including Google, may use cookies to serve ads based on a visitor’s previous visits to this and other websites. Google’s use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads based on visits to this site and/or other sites on the internet. Advertising cookies are only used if you allow them in our cookie choices. You can opt out of personalised advertising at Google Ads Settings, or at aboutads.info.

Technical logs

To keep the service safe from abuse we briefly keep a one-way hash of the visitor’s network address for rate limiting. It cannot be turned back into an address and is removed within a day.

Results are snapshots

A check shows what happened at one moment. Timeouts, blocked requests and DNS problems are not reported as confirmed downtime.
